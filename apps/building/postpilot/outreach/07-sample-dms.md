# PostPilot Sample DMs — "Done" Examples

10 fully-written DMs to fictional prospects in different scenarios. These show you what a finished, ready-to-send DM looks like.

**Use these as study material.** Adapt the structure to your real prospects. The "personalization" is what makes them work.

---

## DM 1 (Pain-point opener) — to a coach who vented about LinkedIn

**Prospect:** Sarah Chen, executive coach for tech founders
**Trigger:** Posted last week: "I've been meaning to post on LinkedIn for 6 months. The 'meaning to' is doing all the work."

```
Hey Sarah — saw your post about meaning-to vs. doing.

Quick question: how long have you been "meaning to get more consistent" with LinkedIn?

I ask because I run a service that does exactly this for coaches and consultants. 3 on-brand posts a week, written in your voice. You approve, you post.

No pitch here — genuinely curious about the 6-months thing. Most of the people I work with say something similar.
```

**Why this works:**
- Opens with the exact thing she said (shows you read it)
- Asks a question that lets her tell you her pain
- Frames the offer as "what I do" not "what you should buy"
- "No pitch" disarms defensiveness

## DM 2 (Compliment + soft pitch) — to a coach who posts but inconsistently

**Prospect:** Marcus Johnson, sales coach for fractional CROs
**Trigger:** Posted 4 times in October, none in November

```
Hey Marcus — your post on "the 4-hour workweek is a lie for sales" was the cleanest breakdown of why CRMs don't fix process I've read.

Curious: do you have a system for writing these, or do they just happen when inspiration hits?

I ask because I help a few fractional CROs do this weekly without thinking about it. Happy to share how if it's useful.
```

**Why this works:**
- Specific compliment (not "great post!")
- Names the gap (inspiration vs system)
- Soft CTA ("share how")

## DM 5 (Free value first) — HIGHEST CONVERSION, takes 5 min to make

**Prospect:** Alex Rivera, fractional CRO
**Trigger:** Posted last week about pricing fractional offers

```
Hey Alex — saw you're in fractional CRO space.

Made you something. Here's a draft LinkedIn post on fractional pricing in your voice:

---

**Most fractional CROs price like employees. Then they work like vendors.**

Three years ago I took a fractional CRO gig for $8k/month.

Today the same client pays me $22k/month.

Same scope. Same hours. The only thing that changed: I stopped pitching hours and started pitching outcomes.

When you price fractional work by the hour, you're saying "my time is the product." When you price it by the outcome ("grow pipeline 30% in 6 months"), you're saying "my judgment is the product."

Judgment scales. Time doesn't.

Stop selling hours.

---

If this is the kind of thing you'd actually post, I have a service that does this every Monday for $99/mo. No pressure on this — happy to send a few more drafts to see if it's a fit.
```

**Why this works:**
- 90% value, 10% ask
- The post itself is good — Alex might use it even if he doesn't hire you
- Specific number ($8k → $22k) makes it credible
- "No pressure" reduces the ask

## DM 6 (Question opener) — pure cold, no context

**Prospect:** Rachel Wong, business coach for consultants
**Trigger:** Mutual LinkedIn connection, no recent activity

```
Hey Rachel — quick question for you.

If your LinkedIn was actually consistent for the next 90 days, what would that change for your inbound?

I help consultants do exactly this. No idea if it's on your radar — just curious how you'd answer.
```

**Why this works:**
- Question forces the prospect to think, not just reply
- Reframes "I don't need LinkedIn" as "what would change if you did"
- Low-pressure opener

## DM 7 (Event-trigger) — timing-based outreach

**Prospect:** James Liu, executive coach
**Trigger:** Just posted "Raising prices in January. First time in 3 years."

```
Hey James — saw you raised prices for January. Congrats.

Quick observation: most people in your position see 2-3x more inbound when their LinkedIn is consistent through the launch. Right now you're going dark after the announcement.

I run a small service that keeps your LinkedIn active through moments like this — 3 on-brand posts a week, written in your voice, you just approve.

Worth a chat? 10 minutes, no pitch.
```

**Why this works:**
- References the specific event (raising prices)
- Names the cost of inaction (going dark)
- Frames it as supporting their decision, not selling

## DM 9 (Direct offer with risk reversal) — confident pitch

**Prospect:** Tom Bradley, sales coach
**Trigger:** Replied positively to a previous message

```
Hey Tom — I'll write your LinkedIn for a month.

3 on-brand posts a week, in your voice. Delivered Monday morning. You approve or edit. Cancel anytime. If your first month doesn't deliver posts you'd actually publish, I refund it.

$99/mo to start. No contract.

10 minutes to onboard. Worth trying?
```

**Why this works:**
- Confident, not needy
- Lists every objection-killer (cancel anytime, refund, no contract)
- Direct ask at the end

## DM 10 (Voice-mimicry sample) — personalized pitch with sample

**Prospect:** Maya Singh, leadership coach
**Trigger:** Asked "how do I know it'll sound like me?"

```
Hey Maya — I cloned your writing voice from your last 5 LinkedIn posts.

Here's a draft post in your style:

---

**The feedback I got last week that changed how I run 1:1s.**

My client told me: "You're the first person who asked what I'd do differently before giving me feedback."

I hadn't realized I was doing that. But I had been, for years.

The pattern: most coaches default to "here's what you should do." I default to "what would you do if you weren't worried about what I'd think?"

The second question gets 10x better answers.

Most leadership advice assumes the coach knows. The better question assumes the client does.

---

Posted this exact pattern would land well in leadership coaching right now.

If you want, I can do this every week. $99/mo, 3 posts/week, you approve.

If not — no worries, the post is yours to use.
```

**Why this works:**
- Demonstrates capability (the sample proves it)
- The sample itself is the pitch — Maya sees the quality before any commitment
- "If not, the post is yours" is the strongest possible risk reversal

## How to use these templates

1. Pick the template that matches your prospect's situation
2. Replace all `[BRACKETS]` with the prospect's specific details
3. Personalize the niche/context to their world
4. Read it back — if it sounds generic, the personalization isn't specific enough
5. Send between 9-11am their time zone (Tue-Thu)

**The difference between a 5% reply rate and a 25% reply rate is the personalization.** Generic templates get archived. Specific messages get replies.
