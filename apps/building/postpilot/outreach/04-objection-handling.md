# File 04 — Objection Handling

Top 5 objections in priority order, with what they actually mean and how to respond.

**General rules:**
- Don't argue. Don't get defensive. Don't oversell.
- Acknowledge the truth in their concern. Then redirect.
- "I understand" beats "Yes but."
- Keep responses under 60 words.

---

## Objection 1: "I can just use ChatGPT myself"

**What they say:**
> "Honestly I can just run my posts through ChatGPT. Why would I pay for this?"

**What they mean:**
> "I don't see the difference. Help me see it."

**Response:**
```
You can — and you probably already have. The thing most people miss is the voice part. AI gives you generic posts. PostPilot gives you posts that sound like you specifically — same patterns, same hooks, same opinions.

The difference shows up in engagement. Generic posts get polite likes. Voice-matched posts get DMs.

Try one month. If the engagement isn't noticeably better than your DIY attempts, refund.
```

**Underlying belief:** They think AI = ChatGPT. You need to show that voice-matching is a different product.

---

## Objection 2: "How do I know it'll sound like me?"

**What they say:**
> "The posts AI writes never sound like me. How is this different?"

**What they mean:**
> "I've been burned before. Show me proof."

**Response:**
```
Two things: 

1. We train on 5-10 of your actual posts first. The first draft is informed by how you really write, not what AI thinks people in your niche write.

2. The first month includes unlimited revisions. If it doesn't sound like you, we keep tweaking until it does.

Want me to send a free draft based on your last few posts? You can see before paying.
```

**Underlying belief:** They've been disappointed by AI tools before. They're risk-averse, not cheap. Risk reversal matters more than price.

---

## Objection 3: "I don't have time to review drafts either"

**What they say:**
> "Even reviewing drafts sounds like work. I have a business to run."

**What they mean:**
> "I'm burned out. The cost of any new thing feels high."

**Response:**
```
Fair. Most of my clients review drafts on Monday morning while drinking coffee — about 5 minutes for 3 posts.

If a draft is close, you hit approve. If not, one comment and we revise. The goal is: 5 minutes a week from you, not 30.

If you find it still feels like work after 2 weeks, we refund the month. No questions.
```

**Underlying belief:** They're not actually time-poor — they're decision-fatigued. Position the offer as reducing decisions, not adding work.

---

## Objection 4: "What if it doesn't work / I don't get clients from it?"

**What they say:**
> "I've paid for marketing services before that didn't deliver. What makes this different?"

**What they mean:**
> "I want proof it'll pay for itself."

**Response:**
```
LinkedIn doesn't promise clients directly. What it does promise: visibility in your niche, inbound DMs, and a credible presence when prospects check you out.

Most of my clients see one of three: more inbound DMs, faster deal cycles (because prospects already trust them), or higher close rates.

I can't promise specific client numbers. But the first month is free if you're not happy. The risk is on me.
```

**Underlying belief:** They've been sold "guaranteed ROI" before and resent it. Honest positioning + risk reversal beats overselling.

## Objection 5: "$99/mo is a lot for content I haven't seen yet"

**What they say:**
> "I need to see the posts first before I commit to anything."

**What they mean:**
> "I'm not sold yet but I might be — show me the goods."

**Response:**
```
Agreed — don't pay for what you haven't seen.

Here's a free draft in your voice right now, no strings:

[Send the sample post using DM 5 from 01-cold-dms.md]

If you like it, we talk about month 1. If you don't, you have a free post you can use anyway.
```

**Underlying belief:** They're price-sensitive on principle (not actual ability to pay). The "show me first" request is a real qualification step.

## Bonus objection: "I already have a VA / writer"

**What they say:**
> "I have someone who writes for me already."

**What they mean:**
> "Why would I switch?"

**Response:**
```
Most VAs charge $1-3k/mo and write generic posts. What they're doing is templating.

PostPilot is different — it learns your voice from your actual posts. The output sounds like you wrote it on a good day, not like a VA using your name.

If your VA already does this, you don't need us. If she's generic, compare side-by-side: send me 2 of your VA's recent posts and 2 of your actual posts. I'll show you the gap.
```

**Underlying belief:** They might not know what good looks like because they've been getting generic content for so long. Comparing actual vs. AI output makes the gap visible.

---

## Objection that means "no":

If they say any of these, stop trying. They're not your customer right now.

- "Send me more info" (never reads)
- "I'll think about it" (polite no)
- "Not right now" (genuine no — ask when to follow up)
- "Sounds cool, I'll check it out" (won't)
- "Let me ask my partner / team" (they won't)

**The right response to a soft no:**
```
Totally fair. Here's a free guide that helps even if you don't use us: [LINK TO A BLOG POST OR GUIDE].

If you ever want to try, the door's open. Best of luck with [THEIR BUSINESS].
```

Don't follow up again. Move on.
