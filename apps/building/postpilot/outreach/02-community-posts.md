# File 02 — Community Posts (5 Platforms)

Five templates, one per platform. Each comes with a comment-bait question and a "what NOT to do" note.

**General rule for ALL community posts:** Give 90% value. Sell 10% (or less). If you can't give value, don't post.

---

## 1. Reddit (e.g., r/entrepreneur, r/sales, r/coaching)

```
Title: Coaches who use AI for LinkedIn — what's actually working in 2026?

Body:
I'm building a small service that writes LinkedIn posts for B2B coaches. 

Trying to figure out what the real problems are before I scale it. For those of you who use AI (or hired a writer) for LinkedIn content — what's actually worked? What hasn't?

Specifically curious about:
- How much time you spend editing vs generating
- Whether AI-written posts convert to clients or just look pretty
- Whether you tell clients/leads it's AI-assisted or not

Not promoting anything. Genuinely trying to learn what the actual friction is. Will share what I find.
```

**Comment bait (post as the first reply):** "For me the biggest friction isn't the writing — it's the editing. The AI output is 80% there but I always rewrite the first 2 lines. Anyone else?"

**Best time to post:** Tuesday/Wednesday, 8-10am US Eastern.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't link to your service in the post or first comment
- Don't DM people who reply (let them DM you if interested)
- Don't cross-post to multiple subs at once (Reddit detects and bans)
- Don't use a sales account — your profile needs comment karma

## 2. LinkedIn (long-form, shareable)

```
I spent 4 hours writing LinkedIn posts last week.

For 3 posts.

That's 80 minutes per post. For content that "should take 15."

I'm a decent writer. I've been doing this for years. So why does it still feel slow?

Three reasons I found:
1. I write for "everyone" — so I keep rewriting to satisfy no one
2. I wait for inspiration — so I binge-write Sunday night
3. I edit like a craftsman — when I should edit like an editor

Last week I tried something: a 10-minute voice training session, then AI-generated drafts I edited lightly.

Result: 3 posts, 28 minutes total. Same quality. Maybe better, because I stopped second-guessing.

The bottleneck was never writing. It was decision fatigue.

What slows down your content?
```

**Comment bait (in the post itself):** "What slows down your content?" (the last line)

**Best time to post:** Tuesday-Thursday, 8-10am. Avoid Mondays and Fridays.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't use more than 5 hashtags
- Don't tag 10 people asking them to share
- Don't post a "follow for more" CTA
- Don't put a link in the post body (kills reach)

## 3. Twitter / X (single tweet or thread)

### Option A — Single tweet
```
I ghostwrite LinkedIn for B2B coaches.

Their biggest surprise: AI drafts are 70% there.
Their biggest problem: they rewrite the first line 4 times.

So they spend 20 minutes on the first line and 5 minutes on the rest of the post.

The hook is the post.
```

### Option B — Thread (5 tweets)
```
Building a small service that writes LinkedIn for B2B coaches.

What I learned this week (thread):

1/ Coaches don't have a "writing problem." They have a "consistency problem." The first time I helped someone, they said: "I don't actually hate writing. I hate starting." Same?

2/ The reason they don't post consistently isn't time. It's fear. Fear the post is dumb. Fear the post is too salesy. Fear the post won't get any likes. The fix isn't more time. It's lower stakes.

3/ The thing that actually unsticks them? Lowering the bar from "post that's worth publishing" to "post that's not embarrassing." The bar is the problem.

4/ Once you accept "not embarrassing" as the threshold, you can post in 15 minutes. Including the AI draft, your edit, and the publish click.

5/ That's why I built PostPilot. Not to replace the human. To make the human faster than their self-doubt.

If you want to try it, first 5 clients get 50% off month 1.
```

**Comment bait:** "Reply with your last 3 LinkedIn post ideas. I'll guess which one you'd actually post." (Engagement play — reply to comments within 2 hours.)

**Best time to post:** 9am or 1pm US Eastern, weekdays.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't post and disappear — reply to every comment in first 2 hours (algorithm loves engagement)
- Don't use 10 hashtags
- Don't pitch in replies (just answer questions)

## 4. Indie Hackers (build-in-public style)

```
# Post: Building PostPilot — Week 1 update

**The idea:** AI writes 3 LinkedIn posts/week for B2B coaches, in their voice. They approve and post.

**Why:** Every coach I talk to has the same problem — they should be posting, they aren't, they feel guilty about it. The market is massive. The product is small.

**Week 1 progress:**
- Landing page live (built in a weekend)
- Prompt system done (Google AI Studio, $0 cost)
- Sent 14 cold DMs
- Got 3 replies, 1 call scheduled
- Zero clients yet

**What I'm learning:**
1. Cold DMs with a free sample convert 10x better than DMs with just a pitch
2. "I have a VA" is the most common objection — they don't realize VA costs $1-3k/mo
3. People who reply are not always the buyers — they're often tire-kickers

**Numbers:**
- DMs sent: 14
- Reply rate: 21% (3/14)
- Calls booked: 1
- Conversion: 0% (too early)

**This week's plan:**
- Send 30 more DMs
- Post in 3 LinkedIn communities
- Get first paying client (the goal)

Anyone here built something for a "feels guilty but won't do it" market? Curious how you framed the offer.
```

**Comment bait:** "Anyone here built something for a 'feels guilty but won't do it' market? Curious how you framed the offer."

**Best time to post:** Any weekday, morning.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't fake the numbers
- Don't delete the post if it's not getting traction
- Don't ask for upvotes

## 5. Slack/Discord communities (looking for beta users)

```
Hey [COMMUNITY NAME] — quick ask for anyone in B2B coaching/consulting.

I'm beta-testing a small AI service that writes 3 LinkedIn posts/week in your voice. You approve, you post.

Free for the first 5 people in this community for 30 days. After that, $99-399/mo depending on volume.

If you've been meaning to be more consistent on LinkedIn but keep not doing it — this might be the unblock.

What I need from you:
- 5-10 of your past posts (or posts you admire) for voice training
- 10 minutes to onboard
- Honest feedback after week 1

No catch, no upsell. Just trying to learn if this works.

DM me if interested.
```

**Comment bait:** None — Slack/Discords hate engagement bait. Just post and let people DM.

**Best time to post:** Tuesday-Thursday, late morning (US Eastern).

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't post in #general — use the appropriate channel (often #jobs, #services, #feedback)
- Don't tag @here or @channel
- Don't DM the whole community
