# File 02 — Few-Shot Examples

**When to use:** Open this file. Pick 1-2 example posts that match the client's vibe. Paste them into the system instructions of `01-weekly-generator.md` under "FEW-SHOT EXAMPLES". Don't paste all 3 — the model dilutes with too many.

---

## EXAMPLE 1 — Story-based, contrarian closer (Leadership coach, "Coach A")

```
Most "leadership advice" is just recycled common sense.

I keep seeing posts that say "be vulnerable" or "listen more." True. Also useless.

Here's what actually works: tell your team the thing you're most afraid to say this week. Not the safe version. The real one.

I told mine we might miss Q3 numbers. Two senior people quit the next day. The other eight worked harder than I've seen in 18 months.

Which worked? The eight.

What's the thing you're not saying?
```

**Why this works:** Specific number (Q3), specific outcome (2 quit, 8 stayed), specific question at the end. No CTA to "book a call." Voice: confident, contrarian, names a specific moment.

---

## EXAMPLE 2 — Tactical how-to (Sales coach, "Coach B")

```
The cold email mistake nobody talks about.

Everyone optimizes the subject line. Nobody fixes the close.

I watched a rep send 400 emails with a 65% open rate and an 0% reply rate. Her subject lines were great. Her asks were generic.

The fix took 4 minutes: instead of "let me know if you'd like to chat," she asked a specific question that required a response.

"Worth a 15-minute look next Tuesday or Thursday?"

Same list. Same subject lines. Reply rate went from 0% to 12%.

The ask is the email. Not the subject line.
```

**Why this works:** Concrete example (the rep), concrete numbers (65% → 12%), one-sentence takeaway. No fluff. Voice: precise, slightly contrarian, ends with a bold claim.

---

## EXAMPLE 3 — Contrarian take (Pricing strategist, "Coach C")

```
You don't have a lead gen problem. You have a pricing problem.

Most coaches I work with say "I need more leads." Then I look at their pricing page.

They have 3 tiers. The middle one is the obvious choice. So nobody chooses it — they just buy the cheapest or feel weird about the most expensive.

I rebuilt one client's pricing last quarter. Cut it to 2 tiers. Raised the high one by 40%. Revenue went up 28%. Lead count went down.

Fewer leads. More money. Less work.

If your pricing page needs a "which one is right for me?" section, it's broken.
```

**Why this works:** Opens with a bold reframe ("you don't have X, you have Y"). Walks through a specific case. Ends with a diagnostic ("if your pricing page needs X, it's broken"). Voice: blunt, opinionated, useful.

---

## How to use these

When you set up a new client's weekly generator, pick the 1-2 examples whose vibe is closest to their actual voice. Paste them under "FEW-SHOT EXAMPLES" in `01-weekly-generator.md`.

If a client's voice is more reflective or vulnerable, none of these fit perfectly — that's a sign to add a 4th example to this folder. Don't force-fit.

## Style notes (consistent across all 3 examples)

- 150-200 words each
- No hashtags
- No emoji (in the example — match client's emoji policy if they use any)
- No "DM me to learn more" CTAs
- One specific number or story per post
- Always ends with a question, a bold claim, or a diagnostic — never "what do you think?"
