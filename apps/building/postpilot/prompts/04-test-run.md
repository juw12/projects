# Weekly Posts for Alex Rivera — 2026-11-02

## POST 1
**Three "yes" replies in a row this month. I rejected two of them.**

The math seems insane. But the math is wrong.

Those two clients wanted fractional CRO work at $12k/month. Same scope I charge $22k for elsewhere. The reason they accepted so fast wasn't because they valued me. It's because they didn't value fractional work in general.

Low-ball yes = expensive no.

I learned this the hard way. The cheap clients cost more in scope creep than the rate difference recovers.

❌ Red flag: "Let's just get started and revisit pricing later."

📌 Green flag: "Your rate is high. Walk me through what justifies it."

What's the yes you should have said no to?

*Strategic angle: Story + contrarian. The "low-ball yes" frame is novel — most posts talk about "red flags," this reframes acceptance itself as a warning.*

---

## POST 2
**Pricing your fractional offer is not a math problem. It's a positioning problem.**

I raised my rate from $15k to $22k last year. Lost 3 prospects. Gained 4. Net revenue up 40%.

The 3 I lost? They wanted a body. Hours in seats. Someone to "own the sales motion."

The 4 I gained? They wanted a judgment. A 6-month outcome. Someone who'd tell them which bets NOT to make.

Same title. Different positioning.

If your prospects compare you to employee salaries, you're positioned wrong.

*Strategic angle: Reframes the rate-increase decision as positioning, not pricing. Targets fractional CROs who undercharge.*

---

## POST 3
**The second client is harder than the first. Here's why.**

Client 1 took you on a discount. Or you took them on a discount. You were both figuring it out.

Client 2 sees what Client 1 paid. Or sees your portfolio. Or your LinkedIn (👀). They expect more.

The trap: you deliver the same work and feel like you're failing.

The fix: write a one-page scope doc before kickoff. Spell out exactly what "done" looks like at week 4, week 8, week 12. Then Client 2's expectations match your deliverables.

Documented scope beats delivered scope.

*Strategic angle: Tactical how-to. Names a specific pain (Client 2 anxiety) most fractional CROs feel but don't talk about. The "scope doc" is concrete and actionable.*
