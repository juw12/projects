## The source asset (original ~600-word blog post)

# 5 Cost-Effective Cybersecurity Basics Every Small Business Should Do This Quarter

Most small-business cyber incidents are not the work of a state-sponsored hacker. They come from the boring stuff: a login page that still works months after someone left, a laptop that never got its last update, a backup set up once and never tested. The good news is that the boring stuff is exactly where the cheapest, fastest wins live.

Here are five basics that cost very little (often nothing) and cut real risk this quarter.

## 1. Turn on multi-factor authentication everywhere

A stolen password is the number-one entry point. MFA closes most of that door, and the big providers (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, your banking, your CRM) all offer it for free. The one rule that matters: no one gets an exception. The founder, the bookkeeper, the intern, all of them. It takes an afternoon to roll out and removes a huge share of the most common breach path.

## 2. Back up, then actually test the restore

"Backed up" and "recoverable" are not the same thing. Use the 3-2-1 rule: three copies of your data, on two different kinds of storage, with one copy off-site or in the cloud. The part most teams skip is the restore test. Pick one file a month and pull it back. If the restore takes three hours instead of twenty minutes, you will want to know on a Tuesday, not the day after a ransomware event.

## 3. Automate the updates you keep putting off

Patching is unglamorous, and delays add up. Most operating systems and popular apps will update automatically, so let them. For the rest, block out a recurring "patch window" and make it a standing item. The same goes for your router and any device that talks to the internet. A forgotten firmware update on an old printer or camera is a surprisingly common way in.

## 4. Run a ten-minute phishing drill

Your team is your first firewall, but only if they are primed. Once a quarter, send a harmless fake phishing email and see who clicks. Do not shame the people who do. Just give the two-minute "what just happened" and a retake. The teams that test themselves routinely see click rates fall by about half within a couple of cycles. It is the cheapest training you will ever buy.

## 5. Lock down who can actually access what

Audit who has access to what, then trim it. When someone changes roles or leaves, that is the moment to revoke. Pair this with a password manager so you are not reusing the same login across five tools. The principle is simple: every person and app should have only the access their job needs, no more.

## The bigger point

None of this needs a security budget or a vendor call. It needs an hour a week and a habit. Do these five things this quarter and you are ahead of a large share of businesses that assume the risk only lands on the bigger, flashier targets. Small is not the same as safe, but it can be a lot cheaper to be careful than to be sorry.

## How I built this (sidebar)

**Source material.** I wrote the original ~600-word blog post from scratch. No scraping, no existing client content. The topic and the five basics come from widely published, well-established small-business security guidance (MFA, 3-2-1 backups, patching, phishing awareness, least privilege). I rephrased everything in original copy.

**AI tools used.**
- A large language model (Claude) for the initial repurposing pass: it generated the first-cut LinkedIn, X, newsletter, and short-script variants from the blog post.
- The model handled the one-to-many expansion quickly, which is the part that is genuinely worth automating.

**What human review added.**
- I rewrote nearly every hook. The first-pass AI hooks were too generic and read the same as a thousand other posts.
- I cut the marketing fluff and removed anything that sounded like a template.
- I fact-checked the security claims against standard guidance and dropped anything I could not stand behind.
- I tuned the voice to a small-business owner, not a vendor.

**Time taken.**
- Blog post: ~45 minutes (draft plus review)
- Repurposing set: ~60 minutes (AI generation plus human rewrite)
- Total: about 2 hours for the full package

**The honest split.** AI did the volume. I did the judgment. You are getting the judgment part, which is the part that keeps it from sounding like everyone else.
