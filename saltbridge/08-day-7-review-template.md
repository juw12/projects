# Day 7 Review — Metrics & Iteration Playbook

After 7 days of having your Fiverr gigs live and your first Upwork proposals + cold outreach sent, you'll have enough data to know what's working. **Don't skip this review.** Most new freelancers either quit too early (because nothing happened in week 1) or coast too long (because "something" is happening but it's not enough).

**Do this review on Day 7, 14, 21, and 30.** Same template each time.

---

## What to measure

Pull these from your Fiverr seller dashboard, Upwork dashboard, and the Google Sheet tracker.

### Fiverr

| Metric | Where to find it | Healthy range for a new seller (Day 7) |
|---|---|---|
| Gig impressions | Analytics → Gig → Impressions | 200-1,000+ (depends on category) |
| Gig clicks | Analytics → Gig → Clicks | 5-15% of impressions |
| Orders | Orders tab | 0-3 (review matters more than volume here) |
| Response rate | Analytics → Response rate | Target 95%+ |
| Response time | Analytics → Avg response time | Target <2 hours |
| Search rank | Manual: search your gig title in incognito | "Top 20" means visible |

### Upwork

| Metric | Where to find it | Healthy range for Day 7 |
|---|---|---|
| Proposals sent | Your tracker | 15-30 sent |
| Proposal views | Upwork notifications / tracker | 30-50% of sent |
| Interview requests | Upwork messages / tracker | 1-5 |
| Connects spent | Upwork → Reports | Track this — burn rate matters |
| Earnings | Upwork → Reports | $0-$200 is normal |

### Cold outreach

| Metric | Where to find it | Healthy range for Day 7 |
|---|---|---|
| Messages sent | Google Sheet | 20-50 |
| Replies | Google Sheet | 1-3 (1-3% reply rate) |
| Free samples delivered | Google Sheet | 1-3 |
| Paid conversions | Google Sheet | 0-1 (revenue $50-300 if any) |

## What to do with the numbers

### Scenario A: Zero orders, low impressions

**Likely cause:** Your gig title isn't matching what buyers search, or your gig image doesn't stand out.

**Fix:**
1. Re-check the gig title — does it match the EXACT phrasing buyers use? (Search "chinese translation" in Fiverr's search bar and compare.)
2. Rewrite the gig title to lead with the highest-search-volume keyword.
3. Redo the gig image — bigger text, clearer value prop, more contrast.
4. Add more gig-specific tags (5 max — use all 5).

### Scenario B: Impressions but no clicks

**Likely cause:** Your thumbnail/image isn't compelling enough vs. competitors.

**Fix:**
1. Redo the gig image — bigger text, single benefit, strong contrast.
2. Check competitor thumbnails in your category — what's different about yours?
3. Verify your starting price isn't scaring buyers off ($5-15 Basic tier is the "comfort zone" for new buyers to take a chance on an unreviewed seller).

### Scenario C: Clicks but no orders / messages

**Likely cause:** Gig description isn't converting browsers to buyers.

**Fix:**
1. Lead the description with a stronger pain-point hook (read the first 2 lines out loud — would YOU click "Order Now"?).
2. Make the Basic tier MORE attractive (lower entry price OR faster delivery OR extra sample).
3. Add more FAQs addressing real buyer hesitations.
4. Make sure the "Message me" CTA is visible.

### Scenario D: Messages but no orders

**Likely cause:** You're not converting conversations to orders. Re-read `07-first-buyer-response-playbook.md` — are you asking for the order, or just chatting?

**Fix:**
1. Reply faster (target <30 min).
2. In every reply, end with ONE clear ask: "Want me to send the sample?" / "Order the Basic package and I'll start within 24h."
3. Quote a specific price early (don't dodge the pricing conversation).
4. After 2-3 messages with no order commitment, close the loop and move on.

### Scenario E: Orders but bad reviews

**Likely cause:** Either scope creep (you delivered more than they paid for) or you took jobs you couldn't do well.

**Fix:**
1. Tighter scope on the next gigs — use the "Requirements" field to lock scope in writing.
2. Be honest upfront about what you CAN'T do — refer out, don't fake it.
3. For existing clients: over-deliver on revision requests, message them first before they message you about problems.

### Scenario F: First 1-3 orders in, all good

**Likely cause:** You're doing something right. Don't change anything major.

**Fix:**
1. Ask every happy client for a 5-star review (timing: right after delivery, when they're pleased).
2. Raise prices 10-20% on next iteration of the gigs once you have 5+ reviews.
3. Start a conversation with the best client about retainer work (use `first retainer pitch` template below).

## Day 7 quick decision matrix

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 orders, 0 messages, <100 impressions | Rewrite gig title + image. Don't quit. |
| 0 orders, 5+ messages | You're doing outreach fine. The gigs aren't converting — rewrite description + tighten pricing. |
| 1+ order, 4-5★ | You're live. Stay the course. Ask for reviews. |
| 1+ order, ≤3★ | Stop new proposals. Audit what went wrong. Resume after 1-2 more 5-star orders buffer the bad one. |
| 1-2 orders, $50-300 | Within target. Don't optimize yet — keep doing what's working. |

## The "first retainer" pitch (use after 3-5 successful one-off orders)

Once you have a happy client who has paid you 2-3 times, pitch a monthly retainer. Retainers stabilize your income and reduce your per-order selling time.

**The pitch (drop into a normal chat, don't make it formal):**
> Hey [client name] — quick thought. We've worked together on [X projects] over the last [N weeks], and I think a monthly retainer would work better for both of us:
>
> - You get a fixed monthly slot at $[Y]/mo for [N pieces] (e.g., 5 listings or 2 emails or 1,000 words translation)
> - I get predictable work and prioritize your projects
> - Anything beyond the monthly slot is at $[per-piece rate]
>
> Want to try it for a month? I can send a 1-page scope doc.

**Pricing rule:** Retainer rate should be ~10-15% below your per-piece equivalent (the buyer gets a discount for commitment; you get certainty). Example: $40/listing × 5 listings/mo = $200/mo equivalent; offer retainer at $170/mo.

## When to quit a track (and when not to)

**Don't quit at Day 7** even if numbers are bad. Day 7 is too early to know anything.

**Consider pivoting at Day 14-21** if:
- Zero orders AND zero serious inquiries (not just "I'm interested" but actual scope discussions)
- Response rate under 50% on Fiverr (means your profile looks inactive)
- Upwork interview rate under 5% of proposals sent (means your proposals aren't landing)

**Pivot options** (don't fully quit, just shift energy):
- Drop the lowest-converting service, double down on the highest
- Switch the channel mix (more cold outreach, less Upwork — or vice versa)
- Niche down further (e.g., translation → only fintech translation)

**Don't pivot:**
- Pricing too low (raise instead — race-to-bottom never wins)
- Competing on volume in a saturated niche (re-niche down, don't add more)
- Adding a 4th service when the first 3 haven't sold yet (finish what you started)
