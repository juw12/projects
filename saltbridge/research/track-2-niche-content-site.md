## 2. Recommended niche

**Bilingual AI-tools review site** — the one place that reviews Western *and* Chinese AI tools side by side for EN- and 中文-speaking professionals.

**Justification (~200 words):**
Of the five, a bilingual AI-tools review site best fits this owner for four reasons. **The wedge is real and defensible:** every site currently ranking for "best AI tools" (ReviewRamble, CompareThe.ai, ToolRankers, TechRadar, WriteTested) is English-only, and none covers Chinese models — DeepSeek, Kimi, 通义千问/Qwen, MiniMax, 可灵 — or answers "which should I actually use?" A writer living in both languages can solve a question no pure-EN or pure-中文 competitor can, and it is not a translation play — the *comparisons* are the original content. **Content velocity:** AI tools launch and reprice constantly, which is exactly the steady stream of fresh, first-hand material a new site needs to get indexed and earn authority — no inventory, no on-ground shoots, no paid tools (most have free tiers to review from). **Monetization compounds:** the niche's affiliates are SaaS with recurring commissions (Jasper 25%, Copy.ai 45%, Writesonic 20% for 12 months), so each referred user keeps paying — a genuine compounding asset, not one-off bounties. **It stacks with Track #1:** the freelance/agency audience is a ready-made first readership, and the bilingual reviews double as portfolio proof. Personal finance for the diaspora is a credible runner-up (low competition, high RPM) but carries a higher accuracy/compliance bar and slower trust-building for a $0, 1–2 hr/day start. AI tools wins on speed-to-first-revenue and defensibility.

## 3. Site identity

**Domain options** (brandable; verify availability via WHOIS/registrar before purchase — I can't check live):
1. **ToolBridge.ai** — 中文: 工具桥 — "the bridge between Western and Chinese AI tools." `.ai` is on-brand.
2. **DualLensAI.com** — 中文: 双镜 — "two languages, one review."
3. **TheAICompass.com** — 中文: AI 指南针 — "guides readers through the AI tool maze."

**Positioning statement:** *The bilingual review hub where EN- and 中文-speaking professionals pick the right AI tool — comparing Western and Chinese models side by side, with hands-on tests and honest pricing.*

**Content pillars:**
1. **Single-tool reviews** — hands-on, from free tiers, EN + 中文 tools.
2. **Head-to-head comparisons** — including CN-vs-EN (the wedge).
3. **"Best X for Y" picks** — by role: freelancer, marketer, developer, student.
4. **Beginner guides & buying frameworks** — top-of-funnel, internal-linking hubs.
5. **中文-language hub** — localize top reviews under a `/zh/` path for the CN audience.

**CMS recommendation — Astro (static) on a free host** (Cloudflare Pages / Netlify): free ($0, fits budget), Markdown-native (a writer's natural workflow), built-in i18n (`/en/`, `/zh/`), full control over SEO metadata + JSON-LD schema (critical for review/comparison rich results), and fast Core Web Vitals that help ranking. *Alternative:* self-hosted WordPress (Rank Math) if a visual admin panel is preferred — but it needs a ~$3–5/mo host, breaking the $0 constraint.

## 7. Risk callouts

1. **Google Helpful Content / site-reputation risk.** AI-topic sites are HCU targets. Mass-publishing thin or AI-generated content without genuine testing triggers traffic suppression. *Mitigation:* hand-written, first-hand screenshots/tests, real opinions; don't out-publish your ability to add original value.
2. **Too-broad head terms.** "Best AI tools" is owned by TechRadar/Hostinger-level authority a new site can't out-rank. *Mitigation:* target long-tail + the bilingual wedge ("DeepSeek vs ChatGPT," "best Chinese AI tools," CN-vs-EN comparisons) where big sites don't compete.
3. **Product & program churn.** AI tools reprice, change, or die fast; affiliate programs change terms without notice — already observed (Writesonic dropped 30%→20%; Notion changed and paused). *Mitigation:* update reviews quarterly, diversify across 4+ programs, never depend on one; keep a content changelog so updates are cheap.
4. *(Bonus) Bilingual technical SEO.* Misconfigured `hreflang`/`/zh/` or ignoring that Google (not Baidu) is the realistic index can waste the whole wedge. *Mitigation:* clean `hreflang`, one language per URL, test in Search Console early.
