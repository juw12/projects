# Digital Products Brief — Bilingual (EN/中文) Side-Gig

**Context:** $0 capital, 1–2 hrs/day, background prep (not launching now). Owner is an active freelance EN/中文 writer/translator. Everything ships on free tiers. Sales are unpredictable — plan for the first product to underperform; the real compounding asset is a Gumroad account, a few reviews, and a buyer email list, not the first 10 sales.

**Note on validation:** The `searxng-search` server was down during this session (all upstream engines suspended/timeout/CAPTCHA), so I validated the market with the Exa web-search tool instead. Price points below are real, live listings I verified; where I could not verify a sale I say so. Nothing is fabricated.

## 3 Product Ideas

### 1. Bilingual Email Swipe File (EN + 中文)
- **Format:** Template pack (Notion database + PDF + plain-text).
- **Target buyer:** Freelancers, coaches, and small business owners who must email clients in both English and Chinese but hate writing from scratch.
- **Problem it solves:** Blank-page paralysis on bilingual outreach, follow-ups, sales, and service emails; no one publishes a *paired* EN+中文 swipe file.
- **Production effort:** ~20–25 hrs (50 templates × 2 languages + 50 subject lines + a how-to page).
- **One-time price range:** $12–$19 (market for EN-only swipe files runs $6.99–$27, mostly $8–$15; bilingual supports the premium end).
- **Sales channel:** Gumroad.

### 2. Cross-Border Seller OS (Notion / Google Sheets) for Chinese Sellers
- **Format:** Template / pack (connected Notion databases, or a structured Sheets workbook).
- **Target buyer:** Solo Chinese cross-border sellers (Amazon/Shopee/TikTok Shop) juggling listings, inventory, suppliers, and profit across marketplaces in mixed EN/中文.
- **Problem it solves:** Sellers track inventory in a spreadsheet, orders in a dashboard, suppliers in email — nothing connects, and real profit is invisible.
- **Production effort:** ~30–50 hrs (design databases, formulas for margin/reorder, sample data, a setup guide). Needs real Notion/Sheets fluency.
- **One-time price range:** $19–$39 (verified competitors: Notomantra "Ecommerce OS" $19.50; multiple seller-OS templates in the $20–$50 band; most sit on the Notion marketplace).
- **Sales channel:** Gumroad + Notion marketplace (the marketplace is where buyers of this exact product already browse).

### 3. Translation Memory & Glossary Template for Repeat Clients
- **Format:** Template (Sheets/Notion workbook + a short written guide).
- **Target buyer:** Professional EN↔中文 translators and cross-border sellers who translate their own listings and need term consistency across repeat clients/SKUs.
- **Problem it solves:** Inconsistent terminology across projects, no reusable phrase memory, re-doing the same translations. Existing tools (Smartling, Lokalise, memoQ, Phrase, AI Glot, nocutoff) are recurring SaaS — overkill and expensive for a solo operator. A one-time, $0-tool template that keeps glossary + translation memory + listing field-mapping in one file is a genuine gap; I could not find a cheap standalone product filling it, only SaaS and blog posts.
- **Production effort:** ~20–30 hrs (build glossary + TM + field-mapping sheets, write the guide).
- **One-time price range:** $15–$29.
- **Sales channel:** Gumroad (buyer audience is narrower and harder to reach — a distribution risk, not a market-size risk).

**Excluded from the top 3:** *AI prompt pack* — real but very crowded ($9–$49, many listings at 0 sales), trivially replicable, low defensibility. *Mini-course: "Amazon listings that convert"* — demand is real (verified), but a course is the wrong first product: highest build effort, and rules here say no courses first. Keep both as later ideas.

## Recommended Product to Build First

**The Bilingual Email Swipe File (EN + 中文).**

Market signal is the strongest of the three: I verified at least seven live Gumroad email-swipe-file sellers ranging $6.99–$27, which proves buyers already pay for exactly this category — you are entering a proven market, not inventing a demand. The differentiator is the paired EN+中文 format; the verified competitors are English-only, and the owner is natively bilingual, so this is a real edge rather than a me-too clone. Build cost is the lowest option (~20–25 hrs), which is the decisive factor for a $0 / 1–2 hrs/day constraint — it ships in two to three weeks without touching Notion's formula-heavy features that make the seller-OS template riskier to build well. Distribution fit is the best of the three: the owner's existing freelance EN/中文 client base *is* the target buyer, so the first launch goes to a warm audience with zero paid ads and zero cold-audience-building — the single biggest reason digital products flop. Defensibility is medium (the bilingual niche is narrow enough to own on Gumroad, though the format is copyable), and repeat-purchase on the swipe file itself is low — but that is acceptable for an entry product, because it is the on-ramp: it seeds a Gumroad account, the first reviews, and a buyer email list, and those buyers are the exact cross-sell audience for the $39 seller-OS template in product #2. In short: lowest risk, fastest to ship, best-leveraged distribution, and it builds the infrastructure every later product depends on.

## MVP Scope (Bilingual Email Swipe File v1)

Ship this, and nothing fancier.

**File / page list (v1):**
1. Notion database, one row per template, columns: `Category` · `EN Subject` · `EN Body` · `中文 Subject` · `中文 Body` · `When to use` · `Personalization tip`.
2. 10 categories × 5 templates = 50 templates: Cold outreach · Follow-up · Sales/Offer · Welcome · Cart recovery · Referral · Testimonial · Objection handling · Customer service · Networking.
3. `How to use` page (how to duplicate, how to fill [brackets], EN↔中文 tone notes).
4. `Before you send` page (deliverability + a plain-language ethics note: CAN-SPAM / CASL / GDPR — "educational, not legal advice").
5. A **PDF** export of all 50 (for buyers who won't use Notion).
6. A **plain-text `.txt`** copy-paste version.
Deliverable = Gumroad ZIP (PDF + txt) + a Notion duplicate link in the description.

**Copy requirements:** Write English first (you are the native-side writer), then translate to 中文 with *tone* matching, not literal translation. Every template is fill-in-the-blank with `[brackets]`; every one ships with a subject line and a one-line "when to use." No filler; 50 sharp templates beat 150 padded ones.

**Image / design needs:** Minimal — one clean cover (Canva free tier or a plain title block) and a 3-template preview screenshot. Do not spend more than ~30 minutes on visuals; this product sells on copy.

**Payment integration:** **Gumroad** (free tier to start; ~10%+ fee, simplest setup, buyer-protection handles refunds). Alternative: **Lemon Squeezy** if you expect meaningful EU/US sales, because it handles global VAT/sales-tax remittance for you — worth the slightly higher fee once volume justifies it. Start on Gumroad, move later if tax handling becomes the pain point.

**Hosting decision:** None. The Notion database lives on the free Notion plan (shared via a duplicate link); the PDF and `.txt` live in Gumroad. No website, no domain, no server, no monthly cost.

## Launch Checklist (build → first sale)

1. Lock the 10 categories and the 50-template scope; write a one-page brief.
2. Write all 50 EN templates + 50 subject lines (fill-in-the-blank, `[brackets]`).
3. Translate all 50 to 中文 (tone-matched); have one native 中文 reader spot-check 10 before finalizing.
4. Build the Notion database + `How to use` + `Before you send` pages; add realistic sample rows.
5. Export the PDF and the plain-text `.txt` copy-paste version.
6. Set up the Gumroad product: free tier, price, cover image, 3-sentence description, FAQ, refund policy.
7. Write the sales-page copy and carve out 3 sample templates as a free teaser in the description (lead magnet).
8. Prep distribution (no ads): a short post for your freelance client list, one LinkedIn post, and one bilingual small-business group.
9. Give 3 templates to 2–3 friendly bilingual small-business owners; collect feedback, fix issues, ask for a short testimonial/screenshot.
10. Launch to the warm audience first (existing clients/contacts), collect the first reviews, then push to the public channels — and log the first sale.

## Pricing & Packaging

- **Base price:** **$14.99 one-time.** Sits at the premium end of the verified $7–$27 range, justified purely by the paired EN+中文 format.
- **Upsell 1 (same launch):** *Bilingual Client Comms Pack* — swipe file + EN/中文 quote, invoice, and client-onboarding email templates. **$29** (everything-for-a-bit-more).
- **Upsell 2 (post-purchase, later):** Cross-sell the *Cross-Border Seller OS* template ($39) to swipe-file buyers via a one-line post-purchase email — this is the repeat-purchase/bundle path that makes the low-repeat base product worth building.
- **Refund policy note:** Offer a clear **7–14 day, no-questions-asked** window for a genuine problem. Gumroad's buyer protection runs the mechanics, so keep the written policy simple and actually honor it — for a $15 product, fast refunds buy trust and reviews, which are the only durable growth asset here.

## Risk Callouts

1. **Building before validating (biggest risk).** The EN swipe-file market is crowded; bilingual is an edge, not guaranteed demand. Mitigate: write the full set, but pre-sell/validate with the free 3-template teaser and 2–3 warm-recipient tests *before* polishing all 50. If the teaser gets no pull, you've saved ~15 hrs.
2. **Overspending on design.** A swipe file sells on copy, not chrome. Free Notion + Canva free tier is enough; a 10-hour cover redesign is money (time) burned on something buyers don't open first.
3. **No distribution plan.** The #1 killer of digital products is "no one can find me." The only durable channel here is the existing freelance client network plus one consistent platform (LinkedIn). Plan distribution *before* launch — never assume Gumroad's own discovery will fill the gap. And accept the stated reality: the first product may simply not sell. Treat it as buying an account, a few reviews, and an email list, then let product #2 (the higher-value template) carry the revenue.
