## Before day 1: 30 minutes of setup

Do these in order. Don't skip ahead.

1. **Gmail address dedicated to freelance work.** `yourname.copy@gmail.com` or similar. Keeps client mail out of your personal inbox.
2. **Fiverr account** — sign up with the new Gmail, complete the "Become a Seller" onboarding to 100%. Skipping profile steps tanks your search ranking.
3. **Upwork account** — same Gmail, complete the profile wizard, connect a payment method (PayPal or direct bank).
4. **Payment-ready.** Confirm PayPal OR a bank account that can receive USD/EUR transfers. Fiverr pays to PayPal/bank; Upwork pays to PayPal/Wise/direct bank.
5. **Pick a business name or use your real name.** A real name is fine and actually converts better for personal-service gigs. Don't overthink this.
6. **Google Sheet for outreach tracking.** Columns: brand | channel | handle | product_url | date_sent | status | followup_date | notes. Share it to your phone.
7. **Three headshots.** Fiverr wants approachable (warm light, slight smile, neutral background). Upwork wants professional (clean, slightly more formal). Don't reuse the same photo.

That's the entire pre-launch setup. No paid tools. No inventory. No capital.

## Common mistakes (don't do these)

1. **Pricing too low to "win" races you don't want.** Race-to-bottom clients churn in 1-2 orders.
2. **Slow responses.** Fiverr tracks response time. If you take >24 hours consistently, your gigs sink.
3. **Skipping profile completion steps.** Fiverr and Upwork both punish incomplete profiles with worse search placement.
4. **Generic copy-paste proposals.** Buyer can tell. The [bracketed fields] in the templates are not optional.
5. **Quitting after 2 weeks of silence.** Median first-sale time for new freelancers is 3-4 weeks. Don't quit at week 2.
6. **Trying every platform at once.** Fiverr + Upwork + cold outreach is enough. Don't add PeoplePerHour, Freelancer.com, etc.
