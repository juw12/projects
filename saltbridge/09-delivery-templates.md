# Delivery Templates — What Great Output Looks Like

The moment a buyer sends a real project, the deliverable itself is what gets you a 5-star review or a 3-star one. Most new freelancers send a plain text file with the translation and nothing else. That's the 3-star path.

The 5-star path: deliver the work + a per-project glossary + a short rationale note + a clean format the buyer can drop into their workflow.

Below: the format for each of the 3 gigs in `01-fiverr-gigs.md`. When a buyer sends a project, copy the matching template and fill it in. Paste your draft into this session first if you want me to review.

---

## Translation deliverable

**File naming convention:**
```
[client-company]_[content-type]_[date]_EN-ZH.docx
example: acme-saas_homepage_2026-11-01_EN-ZH.docx
```

**Document structure (4 sections):**

### 1. The translation (top of file)
- Original on the left, translation on the right (table format), OR
- Just the translation, with original-language terms in `[brackets]` where the buyer needs to confirm terminology choices (e.g., `[品牌名]` for a brand name they need to verify is consistent across their site)
- Preserve all formatting: headings, bullets, bold, links

### 2. Terminology choices (1-paragraph note)
A short note covering:
- Terms where you chose one variant over another (e.g., "Used 软件 not 軟體 — target market is mainland China")
- Brand names — kept in original English where pronunciation matters (Apple, BMW), translated where it's a generic term
- Idiomatic phrases you restructured (e.g., "Your 'we're here to help' became 我们随时为您提供帮助 — direct and warm, fits mainland customer service register")

### 3. Glossary (table, ~5-20 rows)
```
| English | 中文 | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Onboarding | 入门引导 | vs. 注册流程 (registration flow) — used for first-time user setup |
| Workflow | 工作流 | locked for consistency with your existing site |
| [your brand] | [your brand] | kept in English per your guidance |
```

Save this as a separate file too — `acme-saas_glossary_2026-11-01.md` — and offer it to the client. Future translators working on their content will need it.

### 4. Quick questions / clarifications (3-5 bullet points max)
Anything ambiguous you had to make a call on. The buyer wants to see these — it shows you thought about the content, not just the words. Examples:
- "Paragraph 3: 'industry-leading' — translated as 行业领先 (industry-leading), not 业界一流 (best-in-class). Used stronger claim per your tone. Let me know if you want softer."
- "Footer CTA 'Get started' — translated as 立即开始 (start now). Direct, fits your mainland audience. Alternative: 开始使用 (start using) if you want softer."

**Why this format works:** The buyer can drop the translation into their workflow immediately, has a glossary for future work, sees your thinking on the hard calls, and has clear answers to the questions they'd otherwise have to email you about. **This is the difference between a $35 order and a $300 retainer.**

## Product description deliverable

**File naming:**
```
[client-brand]_[platform]_[number]-listings_[date].docx
example: joyhome_amazon_10-listings_2026-11-01.docx
```

**Document structure:**

### 1. Per-listing block (one for each product)
```
## [Product Name]
**Platform:** [Amazon / Etsy / Shopify]
**Category:** [Home & Kitchen]

### Original brief (what you sent me)
- Photo: [link or attached]
- Key features: [bullet list]
- Brand voice: [premium, minimal, friendly, etc.]

### Title (under platform char limit)
[Final title — keyword-optimized, benefit-led]

### Bullet points (5 max, Amazon) / Description (Etsy/Shopify)
[Final copy, formatted for the platform]

### Backend search terms (Amazon only)
[Comma-separated keywords, under 250 bytes]

### Why I wrote it this way (1-2 sentences)
[Specific rationale: this hooks benefit not spec, this keyword is in the first 50 chars for A9 ranking, etc.]

### Optional: A/B variant title
[Second title option if the buyer wants to test]
```

### 2. Bulk notes (1 page)
If you wrote 10+ listings, add a 1-page overview at the top:
- Tone/voice you established (and why)
- Brand-specific terminology you locked in
- 3 listings the buyer should review most carefully + why

### 3. Upload-ready formats
If the platform allows, also include:
- A CSV with title / bullets / description columns (for bulk upload)
- A HTML version if they use Shopify

**Why this format works:** The buyer can review the rationale quickly (not have to read every listing and ask "why this?"), has upload-ready formats, and can pick which titles to A/B test. **This is how you get asked to do the next 50 listings at a retainer rate.**

## Bilingual email deliverable

**File naming:**
```
[client-brand]_[campaign-name]_[sequence-length]-emails_[date].docx
example: verdana-skincare_launch-sequence_5-emails_2026-11-01.docx
```

**Document structure:**

### 1. Per-email block (one for each email in the sequence)
```
## Email [#] — [Theme/Purpose, e.g., "Welcome", "Pain point #1", "Soft pitch"]

### EN version
**Subject line option A:** [subject]
**Subject line option B:** [subject]
**Preview text:** [the snippet after the subject]
**Body:**
[email body, plain text or HTML]

### 中文 version
**主题行 A：** [主题]
**主题行 B：** [主题]
**预览文字：** [预览]
**正文：**
[email body, Simplified Chinese unless the buyer specifies Traditional]

### What I changed per market (2-3 sentences)
[Specific note: e.g., "EN opener is a personal story; 中文 opener leads with the problem statement — Chinese consumers respond to direct framing first. CTA is shorter in 中文 (立即购买 vs. EN 'See the full collection below')."]
```

### 2. Strategy note (1 page)
Top-of-document summary covering:
- The core promise/CTA that's identical in both versions (so the brand voice stays consistent)
- The 2-3 things you deliberately let diverge (so the team understands the per-market logic)
- Recommended send cadence (per market, time zones matter)
- Subject-line A/B testing recommendation

### 3. ESP-ready formats
- Mailchimp/Klaviyo/Brevo: paste-ready HTML or plain text
- Mobile preview (most readers open on phone — flag any line that's too long)

**Why this format works:** The buyer sees your per-market thinking (not just translation), has A/B subject options to test, and has the strategy note to defend the choices internally. **The 1-page strategy note is what gets you the retainer.**

## Universal delivery rules (apply to every project)

1. **Reply within 30 min of delivery** with: "Delivered — [X words / Y listings / Z emails] attached. The rationale note covers my key calls. Any questions, just reply here."
2. **Don't oversell.** If you noticed something you couldn't fix in scope (typos in source, missing context), flag it. The buyer appreciates the heads-up more than a silent mistake.
3. **Offer the next step naturally**, not as a sales pitch: "If you have more listings / next month's emails / another language pair, just message me — I'll keep your glossary and tone locked in."
4. **Save the glossary** to a folder. When the buyer comes back (3 weeks, 3 months, whatever), pull it up before starting work. This is the single biggest retainer-conversion tool.
5. **Ask for the review at the right moment**: 24-48 hours after delivery, when they've had time to use it. Not immediately (feels pushy), not after a week (they've forgotten). Right after they confirm it works.
