# First Buyer Response Playbook

When a buyer messages you on Fiverr or Upwork, you have one shot at converting them. Most new freelancers either over-pitch, under-pitch, or take 24 hours to respond (Fiverr penalizes slow response — your gig rank drops).

The rules:
1. **Respond in <30 min, ideally <15.** Even if the response is just "Got it — I'll send a proposal within 24h." Speed > polish at this stage.
2. **Ask one clarifying question before you commit.** It signals professionalism and prevents scope drift.
3. **Never negotiate your listed price down without adding something.** If they push, add a tighter revision policy or a smaller scope — don't just drop the dollar.
4. **Move to order, not to more chat.** Don't be the freelancer who has 20 messages and never an order.

Below: the 10 most common buyer message types, what they really mean, and the exact response.

---

## 1. "Can you translate this for me? [attached file]"

**What it really means:** Ready to buy. Just needs a price + timeline.

**Your response:**
> Got it — file received. Word count looks like ~[X] words based on a quick scan, EN→ZH business translation, your stated tone.
>
> **Standard turnaround (3 days): $X**
> **Rush (24h): +$Y**
> Includes [N] revision rounds and a quick terminology pass.
>
> Want me to send a 100-word sample first so you can check the style before ordering?

**Why it works:** Concrete price, two tiers (gives them choice, signals you're flexible without giving away margin), sample offer to de-risk them, no filler.

---

## 2. "What's your rate for X?"

**What it really means:** Comparing you to other sellers. Will buy if your price feels fair and you respond fast.

**Your response:**
> For [X], my rate is:
> - [Format A]: $X per [unit]
> - [Format B]: $X per [unit]
> - Custom scope: message me the brief and I'll quote within the hour
>
> Quick question on the project — is it [one-shot / ongoing]? That affects whether per-piece or retainer pricing makes more sense for you.

**Why it works:** Gives ranges (anchors expectation), invites them to share more (so you can quote precisely), signals you'd structure the engagement.

---

## 3. "Can you do a sample first?"

**What it really means:** They've been burned before. Real buyers, not tire-kickers.

**Your response:**
> Yes. Send me:
> 1. A 100-200 word excerpt of the actual content (not a sanitized version)
> 2. The target language (Simplified or Traditional)
> 3. Any brand voice notes or terminology you want me to lock in
>
> I'll deliver the sample within 24 hours, no charge. If the style works, you order the rest. If not, you've got a free native-grade piece you can use however.

**Why it works:** Asks for THEIR content (filters out people fishing for free work), commits to a turnaround (signals confidence), frames the sample as theirs to keep (de-risks them, also lets you keep the work for portfolio).

---

## 4. "I'm not sure if it's in your scope."

**What it really means:** They read your gig, don't see an exact match, but suspect you're the right person.

**Your response:**
> Send me a quick 2-line description of what you need. I'll tell you within the hour if I'm the right fit, and if not, I'll point you to someone who is.
>
> My honest scope: business, marketing, and product translation EN↔ZH, copywriting, listings, email. I don't do legal contracts, medical, or certified/sworn translation. If yours fits one of those I'll tell you.

**Why it works:** Removes the "will they waste my time" anxiety. The "I'll tell you honestly + refer you elsewhere if not" line builds massive trust. Most freelancers never say that.

---

## 5. "Can we negotiate the price?"

**What it really means:** Price-sensitive but interested. They'll buy at a discount; question is whether it's worth it for you.

**Your response:**
> I have some flexibility on scope, not on rate. Options:
> - Keep my rate, reduce scope (e.g., 800 words instead of 1,200)
> - Bundle multiple pieces for a small volume discount (10-15% off on 3+ projects)
> - Lock in a monthly retainer at a reduced effective rate
>
> Which works for you?

**Why it works:** Three real options, not just "no." The retainer option is a stealth upsell — most price-sensitive buyers are recurring buyers. The bundle option rewards commitment.

**Red flag:** If they want a flat discount with no trade, walk. They'll be the most painful client.

---

## 6. "I have a long-term project, are you available for a retainer?"

**What it really means:** Highest-value message you can get. Convert with care.

**Your response:**
> Yes — retainers are where I do my best work because I learn your voice over time.
>
> A few questions to scope it:
> 1. Roughly how many [translations/listings/emails] per month?
> 2. Is the volume steady or seasonal?
> 3. Do you have a style guide / glossary I should lock in?
> 4. What's your budget range per month?
>
> If we align, I'll send a 1-page scope doc for you to sign off on. Most of my retainer clients start at $[X]/mo for [N] pieces and adjust from there.

**Why it works:** Confirms interest, asks the 4 questions that actually matter, names a starting price (anchors), promises a scope doc (signals professionalism).

---

## 7. "What's your turnaround?"

**What it really means:** Speed-focused. Often OK with paying rush.

**Your response:**
> Standard turnaround depends on scope:
> - Up to 500 words: 24h
> - 500-2,000 words: 3 days
> - 2,000-5,000 words: 5-7 days
>
> Rush available: 50% surcharge for half the time, with confirmation that I have the bandwidth before you order.
>
> What's your deadline?

**Why it works:** Specific numbers, rush pricing (so you're not losing margin), asks back (gets you the info you need).

---

## 8. "Can you start today?" / "Need this ASAP"

**What it really means:** Either urgent (legit) or red flag (scope-less buyer trying to lock you in).

**Your response:**
> Possibly — what's the deadline and the scope? I'll tell you within the hour if I can hit it. If I can't, I'll suggest what I can do in the time you have.
>
> For context: I don't take same-day orders after 6pm [your timezone], and I don't take jobs I can't deliver well. Better to be honest now than miss your deadline.

**Why it works:** Asserts boundaries (filters out scope-less buyers), asks for specifics, frames "no" as professional honesty, not flakiness.

**Red flag:** If they say "ASAP" with no scope and want you to commit before describing the work — walk.

---

## 9. "Can you do this in Traditional Chinese instead?"

**What it really means:** Targeting HK/TW market or overseas Chinese community. Real and common.

**Your response:**
> Yes — Traditional is one of my working variants.
>
> Quick clarification: are you targeting Taiwan, Hong Kong, or overseas Chinese communities? They use slightly different conventions for some terms (e.g., 軟體 vs 软件, 網路 vs 网络) — I'll match the one your audience expects. If you're not sure, Traditional Taiwan is usually the safer default for overseas and Taiwan, Traditional Hong Kong for HK audiences.

**Why it works:** Confirms ability, asks the one detail that matters (which Traditional), shows you know the actual variant differences (vs. lumping all Traditional together). This level of detail is the differentiator.

---

## 10. "[Just sends the file, no message]"

**What it really means:** Either lazy communicator or testing you.

**Your response:**
> Got the file — thanks.
>
> A few quick things before I quote:
> 1. Direction? EN→ZH or ZH→EN
> 2. Target audience (mainland / Taiwan / HK / overseas / Western)?
> 3. Tone (formal / conversational / persuasive)?
> 4. When do you need it back?
>
> I'll send a price + turnaround as soon as I have these.

**Why it works:** Treats the silence as a default ("they probably forgot to write"), assumes good faith, asks the 4 minimum questions, promises a quote in exchange.

**Red flag:** If they don't reply to the 4 questions either, the project is probably dead. Don't follow up more than once after that.

---

## Universal rules (apply to every reply)

- **Mirror their language.** If they wrote in 中文, reply in 中文. If English, English. Even if just for the first line.
- **No filler intros.** Skip "Hi! Thanks so much for reaching out, I'd love to help!"
- **One clear next step in every reply.** "Want me to send the sample?" / "Order the package and I'll start within 24h." / "Reply with X and I'll quote by [time]."
- **Sign off with your name + bilingual tag.** e.g., "— Juw, Bilingual EN / 中文". Reinforces the differentiator on every touch.
- **Set a deadline for THEM too.** "I'll send the sample within 24 hours — if I don't hear back by Friday I'll close the loop." Keeps things moving.

---

## When to walk away

Not every message is a good client. Walk if:
- They want you to start before agreeing on scope ("just start, we'll figure out payment after")
- They want 50%+ off your listed rate with no scope trade
- They're vague about who they are and what they need
- They want to move the conversation off-platform immediately ("let's talk on WhatsApp/WeChat only")
- They're rude or unprofessional in the first message (it gets worse, not better)

Your first 5-10 clients shape your reviews, which shape your gig ranking, which shapes your next 100 clients. Choose them carefully.
