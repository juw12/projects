Five ready-to-customize cover letter proposals for the bilingual EN/中文 freelance track. Each targets a different Upwork job type. **Keep under the 300-character "quick proposal" limit if you're sending the short form — these are the full versions.**

Replace `[bracketed fields]` per job. Pricing is reference; adjust per your gig pricing from `01-fiverr-gigs.md` and the research in `research/track-1-research.md`.

## Proposal 1 — Generic EN→ZH business content translation

**Scenario:** A SaaS company posted "translate our marketing site to Simplified Chinese" — fixed price ~$300, 2-week deadline. Brief: "native quality, not Google Translate."

```
Your site copy needs to land in Simplified Chinese the way a native marketer would write it, not the way a translator would reverse-engineer it. That's the difference between a translation that ranks in Baidu and one that gets bounced.

I'm bilingual EN/中文, both directions, with a marketing-content focus. Recent equivalent work: [product page rewrites for a B2B SaaS going into mainland / pitch decks for a fintech / case studies for a US startup entering Taiwan]. I keep a per-client glossary so your terminology stays consistent across the site and any future translations.

For [X words / your full site], my rate is $[Y] (~$0.10/word) with a 10-day delivery and 2 revision rounds. If you want a 100-word sample first so you can check the style before ordering, message me which page and I'll have it back within 24h.

— Juw, Bilingual EN / 中文
```

**Why it works:** Specific positioning (native-marketer, not translator), relevant past-project placeholder, concrete rate + timeline, low-friction sample offer, bilingual tag in signature.

## Proposal 2 — Technical / legal-leaning translation

**Scenario:** Fintech company posted "translate product terms of service and privacy policy EN→ZH" — hourly $25-35/hr, ~20 hrs estimated. Brief stresses accuracy and confidentiality.

```
Legal-text translation is where the difference between a bilingual native and a near-native costs you a lawsuit. "Reasonable efforts" in English is not the same as 合理努力 in Chinese, and a Chinese reader sees that distinction instantly.

I work with fintech, SaaS, and tech companies on legal-leaning content — terms, privacy policies, user agreements, disclosures. Native EN/中文, both Simplified and Traditional. The work I send back is reviewed for terminology consistency across the document set, with a glossary note attached so your legal team can audit terminology choices.

For [X words / your stated scope], my rate is $[Y]/hr or $[Z] fixed-price for the full document set. NDA and confidentiality are defaults; I can sign whatever your legal team requires before starting.

If you'd like a 200-word sample first, message me the document and I'll have it back within 24h.

— Juw, Bilingual EN / 中文
```

**Why it works:** Leads with the actual risk (lawsuit framing), shows specific legal-content experience, addresses NDA upfront (a real concern for legal content), concrete rate options, sample offer.

## Proposal 3 — Amazon product description rewrite

**Scenario:** Chinese seller posted "rewrite 10 product listings for US English market" — fixed price $150, 1-week deadline. Brief includes 3 ASINs with current weak English.

```
Your listings look great in photos. Your English titles and bullets are what's losing the buyer's first 3 seconds — the ones that decide whether they click or scroll past.

I write English product copy for Chinese Amazon/Etsy/Shopify sellers. Bilingual EN/中文 means I can read your Chinese source and write the English the way US buyers respond to, not the way a Chinese-to-English dictionary rearranges it.

For 10 listings in your stated category, my rate is $[X] (~$15/listing, 1 week turnaround, 2 revision rounds per listing). Premium tier available if you want backend keyword research + A/B title variants per ASIN — that adds $[Y] but pays back in conversion.

If you'd like a free sample first, message me the ASIN or product photo + 3 bullets in any language and I'll rewrite the listing within 24h. No commitment, no catch.

— Juw, Bilingual EN / 中文
```

**Why it works:** Frames the problem sharply (3 seconds, scroll past), positions the bilingual edge as the differentiator, concrete pricing per listing, free sample with low-friction input requirements.

```

I write EN/中文 email sequences for DTC brands launching in both markets. Five emails, both languages, with subject lines and CTAs tuned per culture (your Chinese CTA is shorter and more direct than your English one — that's not a guess, it's a pattern).

For a 5-email sequence with subject line options per email, my rate is $400-500 with a 10-day turnaround. Includes a 1-page strategy note explaining what I changed per market and why, so your team can keep iterating after.

Free sample: message me your product and target audience and I'll send a subject line + opener in both languages within 24h. Judge the style before you commit.

— Juw, Bilingual EN / 中文
```

**Why it works:** Names the actual deliverable (not translation, native per market), specific insights (Chinese CTA patterns), concrete scope/rate, free sample that's actually useful (subject line).

I write B2B SaaS landing pages that turn "what we do" into "why it matters to you, in your words." Recent work: [a Series A SaaS cut their CAC by 28% after a hero rewrite / a dev tools company doubled demo bookings / placeholder for your real example].

For hero + 3 sub-pages, my rate is $500 fixed, 1 week turnaround, 2 revision rounds. If you also need a Simplified Chinese version of the same page (separate cost, same process), let me know — that's an add-on most landing-page writers can't offer.

— Juw, Bilingual EN / 中文
